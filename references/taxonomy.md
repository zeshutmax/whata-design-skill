# Taxonomy (v4)
Use these canonical slugs in tags, searches and style records. Facets: single = exactly one tag, multi = up to max.

## art-style (multi, max 3, curated): The overall visual language of the homepage.
- `minimal`: Lots of whitespace, few elements, restrained palette, content-first.
  - `swiss-grid`: Strict grid, neutral grotesque type, strong alignment, International Typographic Style.
  - `utilitarian`: Function as style: plain type, small labels, tables, rules and system-UI details, information-dense.
- `editorial`: Magazine and print layout logic: big headlines, columns, captions, pull quotes.
  - `serif-editorial`: Serif display type leads the layout, literary and print-like.
  - `newspaper`: Dense columns, rules, mastheads, ticker-like details.
- `brutalism`: Deliberately raw, unpolished or anti-design.
  - `raw-brutalist`: Default-browser look, monospace, harsh borders, little styling.
  - `neo-brutalist`: Thick black outlines, hard offset shadows, flat saturated colors, chunky buttons.
- `maximalist`: Dense, loud, layered, more is more.
  - `memphis-playful`: Geometric confetti shapes, bright colors, squiggles, toy-like energy.
  - `collage`: Overlapping cut-out images, paper textures, sticker-like elements.
- `retro`: Deliberate nostalgia for a past era.
  - `y2k`: Chrome, bubbles, glossy gradients, early-web optimism.
  - `frutiger-aero`: Glossy 2000s tech optimism: glass and water, sky and grass, aqua bubbles, skeuomorphic gloss and humanist sans type.
  - `vintage-print`: Aged paper, letterpress, halftone, old posters and packaging.
  - `retro-computing`: Pixel type, terminal, 8/16-bit, OS-window UI.
  - `seventies`: Warm earthy palettes, rounded type, sunbursts.
- `illustrative` (Illustrative) [deprecated]
- `dimensional`: Depth, lighting and material are the main visual device.
  - `glassmorphism`: Frosted translucent panels, blur, light borders over color.
  - `claymorphism`: Puffy, rounded, inflated shapes with soft shadows.
  - `skeuomorphic`: Realistic materials and textures: leather, metal, paper, knobs.
- `atmospheric`: Mood created with light, color and texture.
  - `gradient-aurora`: Large soft gradients, mesh gradients, glows.
  - `dark-cyber`: Near-black backgrounds, neon accents, grids, sci-fi HUD details.
  - `futuristic-tech`: Precise, technical, product-as-hardware feel, thin lines, data viz motifs.
  - `grain-noise`: Film grain, noise textures, analog imperfections.
  - `cinematic-dark`: Near-black stage with dramatic, film-like lighting: one product or scene lit and glowing out of darkness, little interface chrome. Not neon or HUD details (that is dark / cyber).
- `organic-soft`: Rounded shapes, natural colors, gentle shadows, calm and human.
- `botanical-natural` (Botanical / natural) [deprecated]
- `luxury`: Premium and refined: considered spacing, restrained or rich palettes, high-end product focus.
  - `quiet-luxury`: Muted tones, generous spacing, refined small type, understated product focus.
  - `opulent`: Rich and ornate: deep colors, gold or metallic accents, ornament and dense detail.
- `corporate-clean`: Conventional polished B2B look: blue accents, product screenshots, clear sections.
- `experimental`: Deliberately breaks web conventions: unusual navigation, interaction as art, abstract layouts.
  - `unconventional-layout`: Layout that ignores the usual hero + sections rhythm: overlapping, rotated, off-grid, scattered.
  - `creative-coding`: Procedural, algorithmic or shader-driven visuals as the main design device.
  - `interactive-art`: The page is closer to an interactive artwork or toy than a marketing page.

## mood (multi, max 2, curated): The feeling the page is going for.
- `calm`: Slow, quiet and spacious: soft contrast, few elements competing for attention, gentle or no motion.
- `dreamy`: Soft-focus and atmospheric: blurred gradients, haze, slow ambient motion, a floating feel.
- `friendly`: Warm and approachable: rounded shapes, soft colors, conversational copy, people and faces.
- `trustworthy`: Steady and credible: conventional structure, restrained color, proof (logos, numbers, certifications) up front.
- `serious`: Sober and matter-of-fact: neutral palette, dense information, no decoration for its own sake.
- `playful`: Fun and light-hearted: bouncy motion, bright color, jokes, toys or characters.
- `quirky`: Odd on purpose: eccentric details, unexpected interactions, copy that feels personal and a little strange.
- `bold`: Loud and confident: big type, strong contrast and saturated color; the page demands attention.
- `edgy`: Provocative or rebellious: harsh contrast, raw or distorted elements, attitude in the copy.
- `luxurious` (Luxurious) [deprecated]

## industry (multi, max 2, curated): The field the business works in: what it is about, whatever it sells or how. First tag is the primary industry. What it sells goes in product kind.
- `technology`: Technology as the field: developer tools, AI, design and productivity software, data, security. A bank app is finance, not technology.
  - `productivity`: Notes, tasks, docs, calendars, collaboration and other workplace tools.
  - `analytics-data`: Analytics, BI, databases and data infrastructure.
  - `design-tools`: Software for designers and creators: design, prototyping, video, 3D, fonts.
  - `ai`: Products built around AI models.
    - `ai-assistant`: Chat assistants, copilots and AI agents.
    - `ai-media`: AI that generates or edits images, video, voice or music.
  - `developer-tools`: Software for developers: APIs, infrastructure, hosting, editors, CLIs, SDKs.
  - `cybersecurity`: Security, identity, privacy and compliance products.
- `finance`: Money: banks, payments, investing, insurance and crypto, including the apps that serve them.
  - `banking-payments`: Banks, neobanks, cards, payment processing and money transfer.
  - `investing`: Brokerages, trading, wealth management and personal-finance apps.
  - `insurance`: Insurers and insurtech.
  - `crypto-web3`: Cryptocurrency, blockchain, wallets and web3 projects.
- `consumer-products`: Physical products for consumers, whoever sells them: fashion, beauty, home and everyday goods.
  - `fashion`: Clothing, shoes, jewellery and accessories.
  - `beauty`: Skincare, cosmetics, fragrance and personal care.
  - `home-furniture`: Furniture, homeware, lighting and decor.
  - `consumer-goods`: Other everyday products: household, stationery, supplements and similar.
  - `kids-family`: Products and services for babies, children and parents.
  - `pets`: Pet food, products and services.
- `food-drink`: Food and drink businesses and brands.
  - `restaurant`: Restaurants, bars and food service.
  - `cafe-bakery`: Cafés, coffee shops and bakeries.
  - `beverage-brand`: Drink brands: coffee, tea, soft drinks, beer, wine and spirits.
- `travel-hospitality`: Places to stay and ways to travel.
  - `hotel`: Hotels, resorts, holiday rentals and other places to stay.
  - `travel-service`: Airlines, booking sites, tours and travel apps.
- `health-wellness`: Physical and mental health, fitness and wellbeing.
  - `fitness`: Gyms, studios, training programs and fitness apps.
  - `mental-health`: Therapy, meditation and mental-wellbeing services.
  - `medical`: Clinics, healthcare providers, health tech, pharma and medical devices.
- `creative`: Creative work and the people and businesses who make it.
  - `agency-studio`: Design, branding, creative and development agencies, studios and freelancers, including their portfolios.
  - `portfolio` (Portfolio / personal) [deprecated]
  - `photography-film`: Photographers, filmmakers and production companies.
  - `architecture-interior`: Architecture, interior design and landscape practices.
  - `music-entertainment`: Musicians, labels, festivals, film, TV and streaming.
  - `art-culture`: Artists, galleries, museums and cultural institutions.
  - `gaming`: Video games, game studios and gaming products.
- `institutions`: Public, educational and non-profit organizations.
  - `education`: Schools, universities, courses and learning platforms.
  - `non-profit`: Charities, NGOs, foundations and causes.
  - `event-conference` (Event / conference) [deprecated]
  - `media-publication` (Media / publication) [deprecated]
  - `government-public`: Government bodies, cities and public services.
- `real-estate`: Property sales, rentals, developments and proptech.
- `mobility-hardware`: Physical products with engineering inside: vehicles and devices.
  - `automotive`: Cars, bikes, scooters, EVs and mobility services.
  - `consumer-hardware`: Devices and electronics: phones, audio, wearables, smart home, robotics.
  - `aerospace`: Aircraft, drones, rockets and satellites, and the companies that build them. Airlines and flight booking are travel service.
- `business-services`: Services sold to companies.
  - `professional-services`: Consulting, legal, accounting, recruitment.
  - `logistics-industrial`: Freight, manufacturing, energy, construction.
- `energy-climate`: Energy, climate tech, sustainability and cleantech.
- `sports-outdoors`: Sports teams and clubs, outdoor gear and adventure brands.

## product-kind (multi, max 2, curated): What the business sells, and in what form. First tag is the primary one. The field it works in goes in industry.
- `software-product`: The product is software people use.
  - `saas`: Software used in the browser, usually sold by subscription.
  - `mobile-app`: An app for phones; the homepage mainly drives app-store downloads.
  - `desktop-app`: Software installed on a computer.
  - `api-platform`: An API, SDK or infrastructure that developers build on.
  - `video-game`: A video game, on any platform.
- `physical-product`: The product is a physical thing.
  - `dtc-brand`: A brand selling its own products directly online.
  - `retail-store`: A shop selling other brands' products, online or with physical stores.
  - `hardware-device`: An engineered device: electronics, vehicles, machines.
- `service`: The product is work done for the customer.
  - `client-services`: Agencies, studios, consultancies, law and accounting firms, freelancers: project or retainer work for clients.
  - `local-business`: A physical venue or local service: restaurant, salon, clinic, gym, shop.
  - `hospitality-stay`: Rooms, trips, tours or experiences booked for a date.
- `platform-network`: The product is a place where people meet.
  - `marketplace`: A platform where many sellers or providers meet buyers.
  - `community-social`: A social network, community or membership.
- `content-media`: The product is content: publications, streaming, podcasts, newsletters, courses.
- `digital-goods`: Downloadable or one-off digital products: templates, fonts, presets, assets.
- `non-commercial`: Not selling anything.
  - `personal`: A person rather than a business: portfolio, CV, blog.
  - `cause-organization`: A non-profit, public body, school or campaign.

## site-type (single, curated): What kind of site the homepage belongs to. Exactly one.
- `landing-page`: One focused page for a single product, service or campaign.
- `company-site`: The main site of a company with several sections or pages.
- `portfolio-site`: Showcases the work of a person or studio.
- `ecommerce-store`: A shop with a catalog and cart.
- `product-page`: Dedicated page for a single physical or digital product.
- `personal-blog`: Individual or writing-led site.
- `publication-site`: Magazine, news or media site.
- `event-site`: Conference, festival, launch or time-limited campaign.
- `concept-site`: Made to explore an idea or technique rather than sell something.
- `web-tool`: An interactive tool or app running on the page.
- `template`: A template or demo meant to be bought or cloned.
- `directory-site`: A curated list, library or reference site.

## audience (multi, max 2, curated): Who the page is speaking to.
- `b2b`: Companies buying for their business.
  - `enterprise`: Large organizations: security, compliance and sales-led buying.
  - `startups`: Startups and small teams buying self-serve.
- `b2c`: Individual consumers.
- `developers`: Software developers and technical buyers.
- `creatives`: Designers, artists, makers and other creative professionals.
- `local-community`: People in one city or neighborhood: local shops, venues and services.

## goal (single, curated): The main action the page wants from a visitor.
- `sell-product`: Purchase a product or start a subscription.
- `book-demo`: Talk to the company before buying.
- `sign-up`: Create an account or start a free trial.
- `waitlist-launch`: Early access, pre-launch or notification list.
- `showcase-work`: Get noticed and hired by showing projects.
- `inform-educate`: Read, learn or explore content.
- `donate-join`: Support or join a cause or community.
- `book-visit`: Reserve a table, room, ticket or visit.
- `contact-enquire`: Get in touch, request a quote or start a project.
- `download-app`: Install a mobile or desktop app.

## approach (single, curated): How hard the page pushes for action. Pick the one that dominates; this is independent of the goal (a brand-led page can still sell).
- `brand-led`: Builds memory and feeling: story, imagery and craft lead, the offer is implied, calls to action are quiet or secondary.
- `conversion-led`: Built to get one action: explicit offer, benefit-led headline, the call to action repeated, proof next to it, few ways out.

## color (multi, curated): Palette character. Theme and dominant hues are detected from the screenshot and confirmed by a human; the palette group is chosen by hand.
- `theme`: Light or dark overall.
  - `light`: Light backgrounds dominate.
  - `dark`: Dark backgrounds dominate.
  - `mixed-theme`: Light and dark sections alternate in roughly equal measure.
- `palette-mood`: The character of the palette as a whole.
  - `monochrome`: One hue, or only black, white and grays, in different tints.
  - `neutral-accent`: Neutral base with one or two accent colors used sparingly.
  - `vibrant`: Several saturated colors used boldly.
  - `pastel`: Soft, light, low-saturation colors.
  - `earthy`: Browns, ochres, olives and terracotta: colors from nature.
  - `neon`: Fluorescent, glowing colors, usually on dark backgrounds.
  - `high-contrast`: Extreme light/dark contrast, often pure black and white with little in between.
- `dominant-hue`: The main hues, bucketed from the extracted palette. Pages that are only neutral get none.
  - `hue-red`: Red is a dominant hue.
  - `hue-orange`: Orange is a dominant hue.
  - `hue-yellow`: Yellow is a dominant hue.
  - `hue-green`: Green is a dominant hue.
  - `hue-blue`: Blue is a dominant hue.
  - `hue-purple`: Purple or violet is a dominant hue.
  - `hue-pink`: Pink or magenta is a dominant hue.
  - `hue-brown`: Brown or tan is a dominant hue.
  - `hue-bw` (Black & white) [deprecated]

## typography (multi, curated): What the type is doing: which kind of typeface leads, and how it is treated.
- `typeface-class`: The kind of typeface that leads the page (headlines first).
  - `serif`: Typefaces with serifs lead the page.
    - `didone`: High-contrast display serif with hairline strokes, as on fashion magazine covers.
    - `soft-serif`: Rounded or wedge serifs, warm and friendly, often at display sizes.
    - `slab-serif`: Heavy, block-like serifs.
  - `sans`: Sans-serif typefaces lead the page.
    - `geometric-sans`: Built from circles and straight lines, like Futura or Avenir.
    - `grotesque`: Neutral, slightly irregular sans, like Helvetica or Inter.
    - `humanist-sans`: Calligraphic proportions and open shapes, like Gill Sans or Frutiger.
  - `monospace`: Monospaced type used as a main voice, not only for code samples.
  - `script`: Handwriting, brush or calligraphic typefaces carry key text.
- `type-treatment`: How the type is used, whatever the typeface.
  - `oversized-type`: Headlines at giant scale, type as the main graphic.
  - `expressive-type`: Custom lettering or distorted, animated or experimental type that works as an image.
  - `condensed`: Narrow, tall typefaces set large and tight.
  - `mixed-pairing`: Deliberate contrast between two typefaces, usually a serif and a sans.
- `serif-display` (Serif display) [deprecated]

## layout (multi, curated): Structure of the page: what is above the fold, the whole-page skeleton, how it flows, how you navigate.
- `hero`: What fills the first screen. Describes the first screen only; imagery describes the page as a whole.
  - `hero-full-bleed`: Edge-to-edge photograph or illustration behind the headline.
  - `hero-video`: Looping or autoplay video as the first-screen background or main element.
  - `hero-split`: First screen divided into two halves: text on one side, visual or UI on the other.
  - `hero-centered`: Centered headline, subline and call to action with little else on the first screen.
  - `hero-type-only`: Headline type is the only visual on the first screen.
  - `hero-product-ui`: A screenshot or live mockup of the product interface in the first screen.
  - `hero-3d`: A 3D object or WebGL scene in the first screen.
- `macrostructure`: The overall skeleton of the page. Pick the one that best describes the whole homepage.
  - `saas-stack`: Classic sequence: hero, logo wall, feature blocks, testimonials, pricing, final CTA, footer.
  - `bento-grid`: Content in a mosaic of rounded cards of mixed sizes.
  - `project-index`: Work or content listed as text rows or a table, often with hover previews.
  - `gallery-wall`: Image grid or masonry is the main content.
  - `catalog-grid`: Product grid leads the page, as in a shop.
  - `editorial-feed`: Articles or stories laid out like a magazine front page.
  - `poster`: One screen carries the whole message; almost no scrolling.
  - `split-screen`: Two persistent halves, often scrolling independently.
  - `chaptered-story`: Full-viewport chapters, pinned or snap scrolling, one idea per screen.
  - `app-shell`: The homepage looks like an application: windows, dock, sidebar, toolbar.
  - `specimen`: A type or product specimen is displayed like a catalog plate.
- `navigation`: How visitors move around.
  - `top-bar`: Conventional horizontal bar of links.
  - `hamburger-only`: Only a menu button or "Menu" label; links in an overlay.
  - `sidebar-nav`: Persistent vertical navigation on a side.
  - `floating-dock`: A floating pill or dock, usually bottom or top center.
  - `command-palette`: Keyboard-driven (⌘K) or search-first navigation.
  - `anchor-only`: Single page with in-page anchors, no separate nav pages.
  - `unusual-nav`: Navigation is a gesture, drag, scatter or spatial map.
- `structure`: Composition and scroll direction across the whole page.
  - `asymmetric`: Unbalanced composition: off-center blocks, uneven columns, deliberate tension.
  - `single-column`: One centered column of content from top to bottom.
  - `horizontal-scroll`: Main content moves sideways, by scroll or drag.
  - `long-scroll-narrative`: A long vertical scroll that tells a story in sequence.

## imagery (multi, max 3, curated): What carries the visuals across the page: the medium, not the overall style.
- `photography`: Photographs carry the page.
- `illustration`: Drawn images carry the page.
  - `hand-drawn`: Sketchy lines, doodles, visible hand-made imperfection.
  - `flat-illustration`: Clean vector characters and scenes, flat color fills.
  - `character-mascot`: A recurring character or mascot drives the brand.
  - `technical-diagram`: Line-drawn technical drawings: exploded or isometric diagrams, schematics and labelled figures (often with FIG captions), on dot, grid or blueprint backgrounds.
- `3d-rendered`: 3D objects or scenes as hero visuals, real-time or pre-rendered.
  - `3d-character`: A rendered 3D character or mascot (glossy robot, creature, figure) carries the brand. A drawn mascot is illustration › character / mascot.
- `product-screenshots`: Screenshots or mockups of the product interface.
- `video`: Video or footage is a main visual element.
- `graphics-only`: Abstract shapes, gradients, patterns or icons instead of pictures.
- `no-imagery`: Type and color only: no pictures or graphics.

## motion (multi, curated): How the page moves and responds. Triggers say what causes motion; techniques say how it is done.
- `static`: No meaningful animation beyond default hover states.
- `motion-trigger`: What causes the motion.
  - `scroll-triggered`: Elements animate, pin or transform as you scroll.
  - `hover-effects`: Notable hover reactions on cards, links or images.
  - `cursor-effects`: Custom cursor, trail, magnetic or following elements.
  - `drag-interaction`: Draggable or swipeable content.
  - `click-press`: Distinct feedback or transitions on click.
  - `intro-preloader`: Animated loading or intro sequence.
  - `ambient-loop`: Continuous background motion without user input.
- `motion-technique`: How the motion is built.
  - `parallax`: Layers move at different speeds when scrolling.
  - `realtime-3d`: A real-time 3D scene or shader effect rendered in the page. The library used goes in tech, not here.
  - `marquee`: Continuously scrolling text or logos.
  - `page-transitions`: Animated transitions between pages or routes.
  - `micro-interactions`: Small stateful animations on buttons, toggles and inputs.
  - `text-effects`: Split, scramble, reveal or kinetic typography.
  - `mode-switching`: The page changes theme, layout or palette on interaction.
  - `pinned-scene`: A section holds still on screen while scrolling plays its content: images swap, text steps through, objects move or zoom, scrubbed to the scroll position.

## platform (single, detected): What the site is built with at the top level. Detected by the pipeline, confirmed by a human.
- `framer`: Built with Framer.
- `webflow`: Built with Webflow.
- `wix`: Built with Wix / Wix Studio.
- `shopify`: Built with Shopify.
- `wordpress`: Built with WordPress.
- `squarespace`: Built with Squarespace.
- `readymag`: Built with Readymag.
- `cargo`: Built with Cargo.
- `custom-code`: Hand-built or framework app (Next.js, Astro, SvelteKit...). The framework goes in `tech`.
